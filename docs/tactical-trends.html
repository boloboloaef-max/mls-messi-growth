Home advantage & scoring trends

Home still matters. The margin has moved.

Across thousands of MLS fixtures, home teams score more and win more often. Season-by-season trends show that advantage is not fixed—and that away sides have become more competitive in the most recent records.

Expected goals and conversion ratios stop at 2022, the last MLS season with paired xG in the archive.Static chart · match outcomes and scoring rates are season aggregates.

Across all 4,088 matches from 2017–2025, the home team scored 1.74 goals per game and the away team 1.23. Home sides won 49.0% of fixtures; away sides won 26.2%, with the remainder drawn. That is a clear home edge in results as well as output, but it is an average across changing clubs, schedules and seasons—not a rule for any single match.

The available xG records help separate chance creation from finishing. In 2,504 matches with complete xG, home teams averaged 1.65 xG and 1.77 actual goals, while away teams averaged 1.21 xG and 1.20 goals. Goals per xG were 1.07 at home and 0.99 away. These ratios describe realized scoring against this dataset's expected-goal estimates; they are not a pure measure of skill or shot quality.

The gap has also narrowed in recent seasons: home teams won 51.1% of recorded games in 2017–2022, compared with 45.8% in 2023–2025, while away wins rose from 25.1% to 28.0%. The later period lacks xG, so the score data can show a shift in outcomes but not whether chance quality or finishing drove it. It is a useful signal of a changing league, not proof that travel or venue mattered less.
