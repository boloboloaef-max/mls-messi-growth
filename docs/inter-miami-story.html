The Inter Miami turning point

A superstar changed the spotlight. The scoreline took longer.

Lionel Messi arrived in South Florida in July 2023. Season-level match records put that moment in context—and show why the most striking shift appears in the seasons that followed.

Inter Miami fixtures includes every match involving the club; Rest of MLS excludes those fixtures.The chart is fixed for reading; no zoom or filter controls.

Inter Miami's first three seasons were not a steady climb into high-scoring games. Its matches averaged 2.63 total goals in 2020, 2.62 in 2021 and 3.03 in 2022. In 2023—the season Messi joined in July—the full-season average was 2.79, almost level with the rest of MLS at 2.76. That is a useful reminder: a season-wide average blurs the before-and-after inside a campaign.

The contrast becomes pronounced in the next two seasons. Miami fixtures averaged 3.76 total goals in 2024 and 4.03 in 2025, compared with 3.07 and 2.94 across the rest of the league. Miami itself scored an average 2.27 and 2.52 goals per match in those seasons, respectively. A more forceful attack is consistent with the pattern; the scores alone cannot separate Messi's contribution from teammates, opponents, game state or other roster changes.

For U.S. soccer ahead of the 2026 FIFA World Cup, the record points to both the pull of a global star and the importance of what happens after the headline signing. Inter Miami became a more prolific, higher-event team over time, but this comparison measures goals—not attendance, broadcast reach or the independent effect of one player. It is evidence of a striking change, not a causal verdict.

Coverage note. Goal totals include 2020–2025 completed league and postseason fixtures. Paired xG is available only through 2022 in these sources, so the xG series ends before Messi's arrival. Messi joined during the 2023 season; this chart does not isolate his appearances.
